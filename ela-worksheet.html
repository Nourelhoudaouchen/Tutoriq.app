The Life of a Honeybee

Honeybees are remarkable insects that live and work together in large communities called colonies. A single colony can hold up to 60,000 bees, all sharing one home called a hive. Inside the hive, every bee has an important job to do.

The queen bee is the most important member of the colony. Her only job is to lay eggs — she can lay up to 2,000 eggs in a single day! Worker bees are all female. They build and repair the honeycomb, care for young bees, guard the entrance to the hive, and collect nectar from flowers. Drone bees are male and have just one role: to mate with a new queen.

When a worker bee finds a field of flowers, she returns to the hive and performs a special movement called the waggle dance. This dance tells other bees exactly where the flowers are located and how far away they are. Scientists study the waggle dance to understand how animals communicate without words.

Honeybees are vital to our food supply. They pollinate more than 80% of flowering plants, including fruits, vegetables, and nuts. Without bees, many of the foods we eat every day would disappear. Protecting bees means protecting our own food chain.
